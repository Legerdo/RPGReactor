# RPG Reactor 0.99.2 (in development)

Work since 0.99.1. The full technical list is in the [editor changelog](../editor/changelog/0.99.2.md).

### Changed

- **Path** in the Terrain group paints roads and paths on the ground: drag freehand or draw a straight Line, pick Dirt road, Cobblestone, Gravel, Sand or Paving and a width in tiles. A road follows the hills, nothing grows on it, and a fast drag no longer leaves gaps (the Ground brush fills its strokes in the same way). **Curve** draws a road by clicking points along the way: it bends smoothly through them and is laid when you click the last point again, press Enter or press **Lay road** (Backspace takes off the last point, Esc cancels). **Level the ground under the road**, on by default, smooths the ground along the road and flattens it across, so it doesn't tilt sideways on a hillside; one Ctrl+Z takes back both. A road's **Surface** can be any of the project's materials, with a tint and a scale; the old five are quick picks, and the map's own grounds are there to paint more of one. Changing the look never changes roads already laid. The Floor tool has a Size, 1×1 to 5×5, and a Line of floor is that wide.
- **Foliage** is more your own: a new color paints a new patch and leaves the plants already on the map alone (flowers painted with new petal colors used to recolor the ones planted). The panel shows **Color** (Color, Tips, Glow, petals; crystals can mix several colors, a shard each) and **Shape** (Height up to 12 tiles, Thickness, Lean, Variety; a bush's Size up to 8 and Tall or squat) up front, with **quick colors** for the kind in hand. **Recolor** brushes the look in hand onto plants already growing, **Pick** takes a plant's look from the map, and recoloring everything from a palette moved under **Whole map**.
- A map holds **64 ground types** instead of 8, and grounds that share a material (a road in two tints, say) share its picture, so they cost almost nothing extra.
- **Ground** takes any material (or a picture of your own) as a new ground, paints along a **Line** or over a **Rectangle** filled or outlined, can **level the ground under it**, and fills by rule (**Fill where…**: height, slope, near or away from water, patchy). Every material picker in the builder has **Your own picture…**, which copies a picture into the project's materials (the Resource Manager has a Materials folder now).
- **3D builder audit**: the **Eraser** has its own Line and Rectangle and an **Only** filter (roofs, walls, floors, shapes, rocks); **Blueprint** shows each plan as a picture, stamps at ×1 to ×4 and a direction, and has buttons to move, turn, size, nudge and remove a building; every piece takes a **Tint**; a **fence** a Height; a door's width and casing material and a window frame's material are in the panel; **Duplicate** and a shape's **Handles** choice are buttons; **Flatten** draws a level bed or pad to a height you type or pick; **Lower** can dig sheer sides; Pour and River show and set the liquid (any material) and a river its Flow; a **Biome** can leave planted colours, water and cliffs alone; Models settings sections stay open together; the colour swatches' labels read on any colour; the bar's hint line wraps and fits each mode.
- And the rest of the audit: **Scatter** in Models lays a model every few tiles along a drag, each turned and sized its own way (a wood, a field of stones); a pond's **Level** can be set below its rim (**Fill to the rim** puts it back); **Save this map's look as a biome…** keeps your own biomes in the project; a **pillar** takes a Thickness and a **roof** a Ridge height (flat to steep); the room's **floor** takes a Tint and a Scale; the bar's slot names wrap onto two lines instead of being cut, and stay readable on narrow windows.
- Fences join their neighbours: rails run to each fence beside them, round corners and corner to corner on a diagonal, and into a wall a run ends against, so a fence is laid by dragging or drawing a Line and never turned by hand. Windows, doorways and panes also go into straight (slanting) walls; a doorway there is two tiles wide so it can be walked through.
- Build bar: a window, doorway, pane or door clicked onto a wall goes into the wall where you point, in place of that piece of wall, keeping its material, height and building; a window on a stretched wall sits where you aim with wall left below it, a door opens its doorway as wide as the door, and both look out of the building. A wall clicked onto a window or doorway walls it up. The placing ghost shows the opening inside the wall.
- Making a whole selected building taller lifts what stands above each storey with its walls: the upper floors, the next storey and the roof. Doorways and windows grow with the wall, their openings keeping their size. Stairs and ladders keep their steps, and the bar says so.
- Build bar: a **Draw** card with Brush, Line and Rectangle (filled, outlined or Auto) in place of the hidden drag rules; a slanting wall line is one straight wall, or Stepped as before. A **Level** card (− / +, Q / E) for what is in hand and what is selected, a **Position** card for a placed piece, and a **Height** for walls, glass and pillars (one, placed or a whole selection). Floors and blocks stand at quarter-tile levels, so a slab can sit under a raised fountain. Every slot shows its key, and the Rectangle button lights while Ctrl is held.
- A Database vehicle's starting position can be dragged on the map, in the 2D and 3D views, and the Vehicles entry is updated (Undo puts it back). The map's Events list shows these vehicles under **Vehicles (Database)**, named like "Vehicle 1 · Motorcycle · Unit 3", and the map marker covers the vehicle's Length along the way it faces.
- Database › Vehicles: every Starting Position row says Delete (the first said Clear), and the rows' place, direction and Delete line up. Deleting the first row moves the next vehicle up into it.
- Rigged humanoids can play any motion of the motion-capture library the 3D Character Builder uses. In Database › 3D Models, a clip animation can pick a Motion library motion, and one Add gives a model the whole battle set (attack, guard, damage, death, victory and more). Fleagus in the Demo now uses it.
- Action Sequences: "Used by" names each skill, item, weapon or battler that plays the sequence, and a click previews the sequence as that one uses it. The Preview line names the skill or item being previewed. The Show Animation source "Current Action" is now "Each Action’s Own" and lists the animation each skill, item or weapon using the sequence plays, since a shared sequence plays the using action's animation, not the first one's.
- The Database sidebar's section headings carry the accent strip and band the editor's other section headings use, and all 24 pages fit without scrolling in the Database window's first-open size on a 1080p screen.

### Fixed

- An event standing as a 3D model with Auto size (a vehicle event wearing its vehicle's model, too) shows its measured size on the map as soon as the map is open; it used to stay one tile until the event was opened and applied. Measuring alone doesn't mark the map as changed.
- An event with the Vehicle field and no picture of its own can use Auto size: it measures the vehicle's model (it used to see no model and switch Auto off), turned the way the vehicle faces, as the game turns it. A Database vehicle's map marker covers the same tiles.
- In third and first person, losing the captured mouse while the game window stays focused (an overlay or another window taking the pointer) no longer opens the menu by itself, and Escape while riding gets off the vehicle instead of opening the menu.
- A rifle held in both hands no longer spins, and its holder no longer turns backwards, in the Action Sequence preview and in Classic battles in a Battle Room (Sniper Shot on Fleagus). The chest's shooting stance added up every frame on bodies whose animation doesn't reset the chest.
- A new 3D material compiles once in a game that has run a while; it used to compile twice and wait out a second timeout.
- The Database and Plugin Manager take keyboard focus the first time they open in their own window.
