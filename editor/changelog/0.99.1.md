# RPG Reactor 0.99.1 (in development)

Work since 0.99.0. The short version is in the [root changelog](../../changelog/0.99.1.md). Runtime **20261016.5**.

### Changed

- **Forge › 3D Character Builder** (`src/forge/Character3D/`): `RRHumanBody` blends MakeHuman's CC0 shape targets on one base mesh and fits the eyes by MakeHuman's three-vertex binding, so anything fitted to the base follows every slider. The pack (`data/human.json` + `human.bin`, 10.5 MB) comes from `build-scripts/build-human-pack.cjs`: every base vertex is kept, with the clothing helpers' faces (tights, skirt, hair, teeth, tongue, lashes), and the default rig is reduced to Mixamo's 52 humanoid bones with dropped bones' weights handed to the nearest kept ancestor. `RRCharacterGlb.toGlb` writes the skinned GLB with unrotated bones, pictures, vertex colors and any clips; carrying an animation keeps the skeleton live in `Reactor3D`'s model reader, so `ModelAutoRig` places every marker from the bones. Guide: `docs/CHARACTER-BUILDER.md`.
  - **Sliders and extremes:** `HumanTargets.js` is the one catalog of 86 paired and one-sided sliders the pack builder converts and the body blends (proportions, torso, bust, limb fat and muscle, butt, head shapes, brows, eyes, nose, mouth and jaw, ears). Each has a natural range and an Extreme one. Past natural, `shape` blends the natural recipe exactly and relaxes the extra displacement over the mesh (24 passes), so a target carried four times as far swells instead of folding; the muscle × weight and cup × firmness grids carry each axis on along its own edge, so two extremes add rather than multiply. Muscle past average adds the limb, chest and back bulk targets; weight past the heaviest natural build keeps filling belly, chin and limbs. `MORPHS`: head size, a snout or muzzle (the face block from the cheeks forward moves as one), a flat face, ears tucked away.
  - **Garments** (`CharacterOutfit.js`, `CharacterWardrobe.js`): a garment is a piece of the tights or skirt helper (or the skin, for gloves and head pieces), cut exactly along smooth lines measured on the average body's chains (spine by height, arms and legs along their bones, angle about each), lifted, draped (smoothed, held off the body), flared, with a rim folding back to the skin. The tights are bound to the skin as MakeHuman fits clothes (nearest triangle, barycentric weights, normal height), and every piece is pushed out of the skin last, so clothing follows any slider. The covered skin is left out. 47 garments in four groups (Everyday, Fantasy, Armor, Sci-fi; plates split into lames), 29 outfit presets, one garment per slot.
  - **One outfit picture** (`CharacterAtlas.js`): each piece's charts (one per chain it covers) lie flat by the chain's own measure and are shelf-packed into a 1024 picture; every texel is painted from where it lies on the body (stripes, plaid, camo, panel lines, chain mail, scales, quilting, studs, trims, grain). Pieces of one finish merge into one part. The layout depends on the outfit only and is kept while the body changes (a slider change on a full knight: about 35 ms).
  - **Accessories** (`CharacterProps.js`): 30 generated from landmarks measured on the shaped body (eyes, the head's section at the brow, ears, neck, back): eyewear, hats, crown, helmets, jewelry, cape, scarf, packs.
  - **Species and features** (`CharacterSpecies.js`): 24 humanoid species as starting recipes; horns, animal ears, tails (with a five-bone `Tail` chain that sways in the Idle), wings, beaks, tusks, spines, whiskers on any body.
  - **Skin and eyes** (`CharacterSkin.js`): the skin is painted in the body's own UV layout from the average body's fields (markings, belly, lips, blush, eye shadow); the iris is recolored from the eye picture's own lightness, the pupil reshaped.
  - **Hair** (`CharacterHair.js`): 35 styles, 14 facial hair styles, 7 brow shapes and lashes, grown from scalp samples with gravity, curl and body collision, measured from the shaped body; hidden under helmets and hoods.
  - **Four-legged creatures** (`CreatureBody.js`): 14 kinds on a smooth-union implicit body meshed by surface nets, bones named for the Rig tool's quadruped template; `model.json` gets the quadruped rig.
  - **Fixed, garments torn on a changed body** (owner report): laying a garment kept the last body's surface normals, skin-bound tights and skin lookup in the cache that also holds the outfit's picture, so after the first build garments were laid on the previous body (shredded shirts and shorts after a big slider move). Those now live for one build only; `character-3d-model.test.cjs` builds the same outfit with a kept and a fresh cache on a child, a large bust and a heavy body and compares every vertex.
  - **Looks** (owner: "Claymation"): the view lights like a studio (filmic tone mapping, a softbox environment from `PMREMGenerator`, a warm key with soft shadows, a cool rim) and draws parts by kind: skin with a warm sheen, cloth with sheen, metal reflecting, wet eyes. `CharacterShading.js` bakes ambient occlusion into the vertex colors (Bunnell's disk approximation over every part, a quarter of the vertices as emitters, about 0.3 to 0.5 s): always in the file, in the view once the sliders rest; the eyes are left out. The skin is warmer at the nose, ears, cheeks, knuckles, elbows and knees, darker at the lids, shadowed on a man's jaw; the eye picture's dim whites are lifted; lashes are strands in a cutout picture (`lashes.png`, coordinates along each lid) instead of solid cards.
  - **Pictures in the pickers:** hair, facial hair and brow styles, species, creature kinds, outfits, garments and accessories are tiles showing each option on the character as it stands, rendered by a small second renderer (tone mapping only applies to a canvas) between frames once the sliders rest, cached until what they depend on changes. They wait for the view on the first tab.
